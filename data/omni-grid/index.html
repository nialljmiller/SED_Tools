THE OMNI-GRID STUDY

One combined grid of stellar spectra for MESA: working notes

ABOUT THESE NOTES

These are the study's public working notes. They started on 6 November 2026 and grow as the work does; the changelog at the bottom dates every change. Every number on this page is dated and says where it comes from. Numbers marked benchmark come from a first run on 22 instruments (801 bands); they will be replaced by the run on all 12,259 NJM bands.

THE AIM

MESA's equation of state is not one table. It is a blend of several (OPAL, SCVH, FreeEOS, HELM, PC, Skye and others): each is used where its physics holds, and neighbouring tables are blended smoothly where they overlap (Jermyn et al. 2023).

The omni-grid does the same for stellar spectra (SEDs). It is one grid for MESA's colors module in which

  • each source grid is used where its physics is valid (for example, molecular opacities in cool stars);
  • neighbouring grids are blended where, and only where, they agree;
  • every node can be traced back to the source spectra it came from.

It should be simple to justify and good enough for most uses, so it can ship as MESA's default.

METHOD

1. GUARD

Every grid first goes through SED_Tools' grid guard, a set of eight automatic, read-only checks. One of them is flux conservation: each spectrum's integral should equal σTeff4. Only grids that pass are served on NJM and used here.

2. PHOTOMETRY

Every spectrum of every passing grid is put through every NJM filter, using SED_Tools' band_average_flux_lambda unchanged. The stored number is the ST magnitude of the band-averaged surface flux. AB and Vega magnitudes, and bolometric corrections, follow from it by adding constants:

  • mX = mST + (X − ST), with the offset per band;
  • BCX = Mbol − MX, with Mbol from σTeff4 and Mbol,☉ = 4.74 (IAU 2015 Resolution B2). The radius cancels, so at equal Teff the difference in BC between two grids is just the difference in their surface magnitudes.
  • The Vega system has CALSPEC's Vega at magnitude 0 in every band (Vega ≡ 0).

Before anything is written, 300 random spectrum–band pairs per grid are checked against SED_Tools' own photometry (agreement to about 10−13 mag). The operator applied to Vega must also reproduce each band's published Vega zero point. The tables are browsable on the archive page: open a grid's PHOTOMETRY, or an instrument's DETAILS.

3. COMPARISON MAPS

For a pair of grids A and B, ΔBC = BCA − BCB is measured at each of A's points (Teff, log g, [M/H]), band by band. B's value comes from its own node when one coincides. Otherwise B's band fluxes are interpolated linearly inside a cell of B whose corners all hold spectra, so interpolation never crosses a hole in B. A band counts only where both spectra cover it fully. [M/H] is compared at its nominal value, as a MESA user would choose a model, so the grids' different solar mixtures are part of the measured difference.

Each point is classed by its worst |ΔBC| over a set of standard bands: ≤ 0.01, ≤ 0.03, ≤ 0.1 or > 0.1 mag. A noise floor (each node of B predicted from its two Teff neighbours) shows how much of a difference interpolation alone could cause.

4. REGION BOUNDARIES AND BLENDS

Not started. The maps will show where each grid can be trusted and where neighbouring grids agree well enough to blend. The boundaries and blend zones will be chosen from that evidence.

NEXTGEN2 VS KURUCZ2003ALL (α = 0)

The two grids never agree within 0.01 mag in the standard broadbands, anywhere they overlap.

  • Where they come closest: leaving out U and u, they agree within 0.03 mag only for metal-poor ([M/H] ≤ −1) F and G dwarfs, at about 5600–7000 K and log g 4–5.
  • At solar metallicity: they differ by 0.04–0.07 mag from 5000 to 10,000 K, with NextGen2 redder at the same Teff. Both grids conserve flux, so this is a difference in colour, not in normalisation.
  • Below 5000 K: the difference is 0.15–0.9 mag, where the molecules are.
  • In U and u: they differ by 0.1–0.6 mag almost everywhere.

So a blend between these two grids would sit at about 5000–7000 K, and it would have to absorb a colour step of 0.03–0.06 mag.

The noise floor is Kurucz2003all predicting its own nodes from their Teff neighbours, at double the spacing. It is largest at cool Teff, so there the nodes it has (●) are the ones to trust.

NULL TEST: KURUCZ VS KURUCZ2003ALL (α = 0)

SVO serves both. They should be the same ATLAS9 models, and above 4500 K they mostly are: ΔBC is exactly 0 at most points, and 3293 of 3808 points agree within 0.01 mag, with U and u included.

  • Below 4500 K they are different models. At 3500 K the optical spectra differ by up to 30%, although the integrated flux agrees to 10−4, so ΔV reaches 0.32 mag.
  • Smaller differences in metal-rich models: up to 0.04 mag at 4750–5500 K ([M/H] ≥ −0.5, mostly +0.5), and up to 0.015 mag at 9250–11,500 K ([M/H] +0.2 and +0.5).

So the two cannot simply be treated as copies of each other. This needs a line on both grids' pages.

THE SUN: BCV WITH VEGA ≡ 0

The Sun's model, interpolated to Teff = 5772 K, log g = 4.44 and [M/H] = 0, through the Bessell V curve (Bessell & Murphy 2012).

The models match the observed Sun to 0.001 mag. Published solar BCV values of about −0.07 to −0.1 use Vega at V = 0.03. SED_Tools puts Vega at 0 in every band, which makes every Vega-system BC 0.03 mag larger.

CHECKS OF THE CODE

  • Blackbodies against the Planck function (2000–50,000 K, 16 standard bands): median |Δ| 4–15 × 10−5 mag, worst 0.0015 in U and u. This is the tabulation of SVO's blackbody grid, not the code; the integrated flux is 1.00002 σT4.
  • Vega: its V magnitude is 0 by construction, and the Vega offsets match the published properties.json values to 5 × 10−7 mag.
  • Detector type (photon or energy counting) changes the results only where it should. A flat fλ gives the same answer either way. Swapping the counting type on real spectra changes UBVRI, JHKs and ugriz by 0.003–0.03 mag, and the very broad Gaia G by up to 0.22 mag at 30,000 K.
  • Coverage flags work: the ATLAS9 grids stop at 160 µm, so Herschel/SPIRE has no value in them, while NextGen2, which reaches 970 µm, covers it.
OPEN QUESTIONS
  • Where should the NextGen2–Kurucz2003all blend go, and how should it absorb the 0.03–0.06 mag colour step at 5000–7000 K? Do any of the grids still being checked agree better with one or the other?
  • U and u: the grids differ by 0.1–0.6 mag almost everywhere. Which is closer to observed stars?
  • A suspect NextGen2 spectrum: at 9600 K, log g 3.5 and [M/H] 0, NextGen2 gives ΔU = 0.37 mag, against about 0.09 at its neighbours. It may be a bad spectrum; check it before using NextGen2 there.
  • Solar mixtures: NextGen uses Jaschek & Jaschek (1995) and Castelli & Kurucz (2003) use Grevesse & Sauval (1998). Comparing at equal Z rather than equal nominal [M/H] would shift one axis by about 0.05–0.15 dex. Is it worth doing?
  • bt-cond: should it be added, with its failing points (log g −0.5) flagged rather than dropped?
  • Kurucz and Kurucz2003all below 4500 K: which of SVO's two versions should the omni-grid use?
  • Which comparisons to publish: per-point ΔBC for every band is about 0.3 GB per pair of grids. Publish summaries for every band, and per-point maps only for the standard broadbands?
